# Application criteria: Neel Nanda MATS 12.0

Use this file to select and assess the research project, respect application constraints, and prepare submission. Use [WRITING_GUIDE.md](WRITING_GUIDE.md) for communicating the results.

**Source:** all six tabs of [Neel Nanda MATS 12.0 Stream — Admissions Procedure + FAQ](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1p-ggQV3vVWIQuCccXEl1fD0thJOgXimlbBpGk6FI32I/edit), read on 2026-09-10. Dates and program arrangements below describe that source snapshot. This is a synthesis, not an official rubric; the source supplies no numerical weights or acceptance formula. Our project-specific suggestions are labeled. External linked resources and the live application form were not independently reviewed.

## 1. Core objective

Complete a small, self-contained empirical investigation of an interesting AI safety question. Teach the reviewer something through a clear claim, evidence for and against it, and an honest account of what remains uncertain.

Interpretability and non-interpretability work are both welcome. Research fit matters substantially: technical competence alone does not make an uninteresting or poorly aligned question compelling. All backgrounds and experience levels are welcome; a strong application task can be sufficient regardless of credentials. [Source: Key Details](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1p-ggQV3vVWIQuCccXEl1fD0thJOgXimlbBpGk6FI32I/edit?tab=t.0).

The form answers are read first and used as a preliminary filter. Prioritize them over polishing the report. [Sources: Key Details](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1p-ggQV3vVWIQuCccXEl1fD0thJOgXimlbBpGk6FI32I/edit?tab=t.0), [Application Task Details](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1p-ggQV3vVWIQuCccXEl1fD0thJOgXimlbBpGk6FI32I/edit?tab=t.sa4u6llpnkph).

### Detailed time accounting

**Counts toward the 20 hours:** active work toward the chosen project's goals, including coding, project-specific paper reading, planning and thinking, analysis, and writing the main Google Doc.

**Does not count:** general learning that could reasonably precede choosing the project, generic technical setup needed for most projects, breaks, passive training waits while doing something else, and writing answers to the MATS application form.

**Additional 2 hours:** the detailed section permits these for the executive summary. During this allowance, do not edit the rest of the report or write new experiment code. New graphs or visualizations from existing data are allowed.

**Source ambiguity:** introductory passages describe the extra two hours as covering the summary and form answers, or more loosely the write-up. The detailed section explicitly excludes form-answer writing from the timer and restricts the extra allowance to the summary and presentation of existing results. Use the detailed rule for planning; do not interpret the shorthand as permission for extra experiments or unrestricted report editing. This does not resolve every wording inconsistency in the source.

**Restart:** a genuinely abandoned project can be replaced and the timer reset. The evaluation tab clarifies a total change of direction as one where previous code and findings are not particularly useful for the new project. Do not treat routine continuation as a fresh clock.

### Submitting existing work instead

Relevant prior research can be submitted with an executive summary and a link to the work. This includes a first/co-first-author paper, a significant contribution to another paper, or a substantial blog post.

- Estimate the hours the project took you.
- Describe your specific contribution when collaborators were involved.
- Explain relevant research skills if the work is not obviously interpretability research.
- Expect a higher standard than for a timed application; the standard route is preferred.
- Submission without a new executive summary is possible, but the source sets an extremely high bar.
- Prior solo work completed in at most 20 hours can be treated as a normal application project.

## 3. Evaluation criteria

These are qualitative criteria stated in the source. The evidence column turns them into practical review questions, not new formal requirements. [Source: What does a good application look like?](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1p-ggQV3vVWIQuCccXEl1fD0thJOgXimlbBpGk6FI32I/edit?tab=t.qtl0g8o3ozvu).

| Criterion | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Clarity | Can a reader identify the claim, understand the setup and evidence, and see why the conclusion follows? |
| Good taste and research fit | Is the question interesting to Neel, tractable, and capable of producing a non-obvious insight? Is the contribution differentiated from common applications? |
| Truth-seeking and skepticism | Were alternative explanations tested, data inspected, controls run, and limitations acknowledged? Are negative or inconclusive findings reported honestly? |
| Attention to detail | Were meaningful subtleties, edge cases, and inconsistencies noticed and investigated? |
| Technical depth and practicality | Do the code, experiment design, and explanation demonstrate understanding of the relevant tools and willingness to test ideas empirically? |
| Simplicity | Were obvious methods tried first, or was their unsuitability explained? Does each added complexity serve a purpose? |
| Prioritization | Did the project go deep enough on one or two insights while avoiding an unproductive rabbit hole? Were pivots sensible? |
| Productivity | Were feedback loops efficient without sacrificing checks or understanding? |
| Showing the reasoning behind decisions | Is it clear why experiments were chosen, what happened, and how failure informed the next action? |
| Enthusiasm and curiosity | Does the work show engaged investigation? The source explicitly gives this limited weight because it is difficult to judge from an application. |

Prior achievements provide additional, holistic evidence and can help with tiebreakers. The source welcomes non-standard evidence such as useful software, startups, and strong blog or class projects. Explain relevance rather than relying on prestige. Neel also states that he will use LLMs to assist application review.

## 4. Research fit

The source describes a shift toward useful, empirically grounded interpretability and broader safety science. The aim is understanding that matters for safety on relevant models or good proxies, with baselines where usefulness is claimed. A method's sophistication is not a substitute for a worthwhile question.

### Areas explicitly suggested

| Area | Representative direction from the source |
| --- | --- |
| Model forensics | Investigate concerning behavior and causally distinguish misalignment from benign explanations; study evaluation awareness or improve forensic methods. |
| Model biology | Explain high-level model behavior: user representations, conflicting information, concept representations, or unusual generalization. |
| Reasoning models | Investigate CoT faithfulness, resampling/thought anchors, monitoring, or steganography; consider whether interventions create distribution shifts. |
| Pragmatic circuit analysis and automation | Determine when attribution graphs or automated hypothesis generation and validation reveal something simpler methods cannot. |
| Objectively measuring interpretability | Test understanding on measurable downstream tasks such as eliciting hidden knowledge. |
| Model diffing | Identify what fine-tuning changed, especially for debugging data or understanding and improving alignment training; start with strong simple/behavioral approaches. |
| Science of model character | Explain values, personas, self-models, constitution adherence, the assistant axis, or **Value Leakage**. |
| Improved interpretability methods | Evaluate, red-team, or improve promising tools such as J-Lens or natural-language autoencoders in meaningful use cases, with baselines. |
| Science of post-training | Study teacher-to-student inheritance, filtering failures, the roles of training stages, or ways to control what a model learns. |
| Alignment training | Improve alignment far beyond the training distribution; use more than one evaluation when assessing broad generalization. |
| Science of generalization | Explain why training favors one generalizing solution over another, including emergent misalignment. |
| Applied interpretability | Demonstrate useful monitoring, prompt-injection defenses, conditional steering, training-data attribution, or other practical interventions. |
| Selected basic science and novelty | Investigate meaningful reasoning phenomena, steering fine-tuning, filler tokens, or an unexpected new application. |

These are starting points, not a required menu. The source warns that suggested problems have **not** been screened for feasibility in 20 hours and that LLM time estimates are unreliable. Scope down to a question with a plausible first experiment. [Source: Recommended Research Problems](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1p-ggQV3vVWIQuCccXEl1fD0thJOgXimlbBpGk6FI32I/edit?tab=t.knytn7x826kv).

### Weak fit and recurring failures

- Grokking, circuit finding for its own sake, incremental SAE optimization/basic SAE science, tiny algorithmic toy models, or highly theoretical work without a compelling connection to current interests.
- A generic project showing a safety concept has a linear representation, locating heads/layers by patching, or showing CoT influences an answer, without an interesting twist or application.
- Studying only old models such as GPT-2, Pythia, or Gemma 2; choose a sufficiently capable model for the actual phenomenon.
- Starting downstream analysis before checking the phenomenon exists in the chosen model, data, and prompts.
- Claiming a method is useful without appropriate baselines such as prompting, a linear probe, random choice, or a random vector.
- Continuing a doomed project, pursuing an irrelevant anomaly indefinitely, or spreading effort across many shallow experiments.

These are strong negative signals, not a claim that every topic or technique is categorically prohibited. Older successful applications are examples of research skill, not a map of current topic preferences.

### Value Leakage: project-specific interpretation

Value Leakage is explicitly named under **Science of Model Character**. The source suggests investigating why model values silently influence answers, where this occurs, whether the model can disclose the influence or whether it can be disabled, and whether a linear direction can predict and causally mediate it.

The tab links [Value Leakage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14345) and [Gilg et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13339). These are source-provided reading leads; this file does not claim to have reviewed their methods or results.

**Our suggested project screen:** replicate the effect in the selected setting; choose one bounded question about explanation, prediction, disclosure, or mitigation; specify a simple baseline; and decide what observation would falsify the proposed explanation. If using a direction or probe, separate predictive evidence from causal evidence and check unrelated behavioral effects. Topic fit alone does not establish application quality.

## 5. Research workflow and agent responsibility

The source recommends **exploration → understanding → distillation**, with movement between them as the evidence changes:

1. Gather information by interacting with the model, reading data, and running cheap tests. A fully formed hypothesis is not required at the start.
2. Keep hypotheses, experiments, key figures, and findings in a running document. Test competing explanations rather than accumulating loosely related experiments.
3. Reassess progress regularly. The source suggests zooming out every hour or two and asking whether recent work is teaching you anything.
4. Reserve real project time for checks and clear communication. The extra summary allowance is not a substitute for writing the report within the main budget.

The source recommends spending at most roughly five project hours on papers/tutorials and favoring empirical feedback. General preparation can happen beforehand under the time rules. Practical resources include ARENA, interpretability tutorials, model/library documentation, and mathematical foundations; these are preparation options, not prerequisites or required deliverables.

LLM assistance and coding agents are actively encouraged. The applicant must understand the research, design appropriate experiments, and verify key results. In particular:

- Inspect actual prompts, outputs, labels, and positive examples identified by metrics.
- Read the code behind central claims and compare reported numbers with outputs.
- Independently recompute or spot-check important quantities.
- Test cheap explanations such as leakage, a misleading metric, grader exploitation, or a baseline matching the proposed method.
- Record what was personally checked and what remains uncertain.

Forwarding a plausible agent-produced project without adding research judgment or checking its claims is explicitly described as grounds for rejection. Fabricated experiments are unacceptable. Raw LLM-written form answers and summaries are a significant negative signal; write these yourself.

For efficient experimentation, the source suggests keeping models/data loaded in a persistent kernel or session, saving plots and expensive artifacts to disk, and using logged background jobs for long training. These are optional workflow ideas, not application requirements. Specific model and tooling recommendations in the live document are time-sensitive. [Source: Advice on good applications](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1p-ggQV3vVWIQuCccXEl1fD0thJOgXimlbBpGk6FI32I/edit?tab=t.75rygwi582jr).

## 6. Lessons from the source's past examples

These summarize the descriptions and assessments **inside the application document**, not independent reviews of the linked reports. The source says the assessments are lightly edited LLM summaries of Neel's notes. They illustrate judgment under uncertainty rather than guarantee outcomes.

| Example | Transferable lesson |
| --- | --- |
| R1 Distill Diffing | Pragmatic pivots and a useful qualitative insight can demonstrate promise even when a central technique has a conceptual flaw. The flaw still matters. |
| Empathic Machines | Clear writing, convincing limited findings, and acknowledged synthetic-data limitations helped; an expected result taught the reviewer less. |
| What Impacts CoT Faithfulness | Interesting questions, sensible prioritization, and skeptical tests helped; unclear writing and limited output weakened the application. |
| “Wait”, backtracking in CoTs | Productivity and technical breadth helped, but breadth left too little depth. Strong evidence of applicant agency affected the final assessment. |
| R1D1 | A well-scoped project, a sensible pivot, clear communication, and a new insight made a strong application despite a less-original starting idea. |
| SAE Equations | Tasteful problem selection and compelling qualitative discoveries demonstrated skill; its historical topic does not override current research interests. |

## 7. Program fit and commitments

These are program considerations, separate from the research-task criteria. Dates, approximate places, and financial details are as stated in the source snapshot.

| Stage | Dates and expectations |
| --- | --- |
| Exploration offers | September 15, 2026. Approximately 34 candidates. |
| Preparation | September 28–October 16, 2026: three weeks, remote, part-time, largely self-directed learning and mini-projects. |
| Research sprint | October 19–30, 2026: two weeks, full-time, normally in pairs chosen by participants; solo projects are possible. Ends with a presentation. |
| Research-phase decisions | November 6, 2026. Approximately 8 participants, selected largely on sprint performance. Most exploration participants do not advance. |
| Research phase | January 19–April 10, 2027: 12 weeks, full-time, primarily in Berkeley; paired research with approximately 1.5 hours of weekly supervisor check-ins per pair. |

- The exploration phase is an unstructured research opportunity, not a taught course. Expect limited individual mentor time and substantial personal initiative. Most events are described as occurring between 5–8 p.m. UK time.
- A full-time job can coexist with part-time preparation, but the sprint requires full-time availability. The research phase cannot be done part-time or on the side.
- Remote research-phase participation is possible but strongly discouraged; the source mentions London as a possible option, not a guarantee.
- The gap between phases carries no research obligation. Early work with possible living-expense/compute support is an option discussed in the source, not a requirement.
- The source lists stipends of $4.2K for exploration and $19.2K for the research phase, plus research-phase housing support. An optional 3–12 month extension is described.
- The source says US residence/work authorization is not required and MATS can help with visa arrangements. Treat this as program information; individual arrangements need confirmation with MATS.
- Applying to other mentors is encouraged. Withdrawal is permitted; leaving after the sprint begins can disadvantage a partner.
- The source says scholars own their work's IP and are strongly encouraged to publish and open-source under a permissive license. Neel's mentorship is described as personal and separate from his Google DeepMind role.
- Future cohorts are tentative. Prior scholar publications and exploration-only follow-on papers illustrate possible outcomes, not admission or publication guarantees.

## 8. Readiness checklist

This is an internal review aid, not an official scoring rubric.

- [ ] The question fits Neel's current interests and has a tractable empirical starting point.
- [ ] The relevant phenomenon exists in the actual research setting.
- [ ] One or two findings are developed deeply enough to assess.
- [ ] Claims match the evidence; simple baselines and alternative explanations were addressed where relevant.
- [ ] Data and agent-produced results were personally inspected; the report documents those checks.
- [ ] Negative results, uncertainty, and the largest limitations are reported honestly.
- [ ] Time accounting follows the detailed rules; any prior-work submission includes hours and contribution details.
- [ ] The form answers and summary pass the [writing checklist](WRITING_GUIDE.md#10-final-writing-pass).
- [ ] The Google Doc begins with a summary within both limits and is accessible to anyone with the link.
- [ ] The current form questions, deadline/timezone, and submission status have been checked before submission.
- [ ] Availability is compatible with the full-time sprint and research phase if accepted.
